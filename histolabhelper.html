Histology Lab Exam — Tissue Recognition

Bologna · Morphology & Development · G3 · May 2026 — identify → describe → explain

General approach at the microscope

Step 1 — low power first

Look at the overall architecture. Where are the empty spaces (lumens)? Epithelia face them. Is tissue solid or does it have tubules/follicles/trabeculae?

Step 2 — identify landmarks

Nucleus position, number of layers, cell shape, matrix color, staining intensity (basophilic = blue/purple, acidophilic = pink). Stains: H&E standard; PAS for glycogen/mucus.

Step 3 — 5-min oral format

"This is [tissue]. I can identify it by [1–2 key features]. The structure I see consists of [architecture]. These cells are [shape/staining]. Their function is [brief]."

Covering Epithelia

Classification logic

Layers: simple (1 layer) vs stratified (multiple). Stratified = classified by superficial cell shape.

Cell shape: squamous (flat) · cuboidal (square, central nucleus) · columnar (tall, basal nucleus)

Special types: pseudostratified (all on basal lamina, different heights → looks stratified) · transitional/urothelium (dome/umbrella cells)

The nucleus tells you shape: central round = cuboidal, basal oval = columnar, flat = squamous.

Apical specializations

Cilia 9+2 axoneme

Motile, hair-like (~7–10 µm). Hundreds per cell. In respiratory tract → move mucus. In oviduct → move egg. Visible as a fuzzy apical fringe under LM.

Microvilli / brush border

Cylindrical projections, actin core. Appear as a striated/brush border in intestinal absorptive cells and kidney proximal tubule. Increase surface area.

Stereocilia

Long, non-motile microvilli. In epididymis and inner ear.

Basement membrane (always present)

Basal lamina (epithelial origin) + lamina reticularis (fibroblast origin) = basement membrane.

Basal lamina layers: lamina lucida (laminin, integrins) + lamina densa (collagen IV mesh).

Lamina reticularis: collagen I/III, fibronectin.

Epithelium = avascular. No blood vessels within epithelium — nutrients diffuse from adjacent CT.
Glandular Epithelia

Exocrine glands — classification

CriterionOptions
Duct branchingSimple (unbranched) · Compound (branched)
Secretory shapeTubular · Acinar/alveolar (flask-shaped) · Tubulo-acinar
Secretion typeSerous (watery, protein-rich) · Mucous (thick, glycoprotein) · Mixed
ModeMerocrine (exocytosis, no loss) · Apocrine (apical cytoplasm shed) · Holocrine (whole cell dies)
Goblet cell = unicellular mucous gland. Nucleus displaced basally; apical theca full of mucinogen droplets (blue with PAS).

Key gland examples to recognize

Sweat gland simple coiled tubular · merocrine

Deep in dermis. Simple cuboidal epithelium. Coiled appearance in section → multiple cross-sections of same duct.

Sebaceous gland simple branched acinar · holocrine

Attached to hair follicle. Cells enlarge with lipid → die and release sebum. Large pale vacuolated cells at center.

Salivary glands compound tubulo-acinar

Serous acini = dark, round cells, small lumen. Mucous acini = pale, flat nuclei, large lumen. Mixed → serous demilunes cap mucous acini.

Pancreatic exocrine compound acinar · serous

Serous acini with centroacinar cells (pale, small) in lumen center — hallmark of pancreas. Basophilic basal cytoplasm (lots of RER for zymogen).

Endocrine glands — key features

No ducts · Secrete into blood · Fenestrated capillaries everywhere.

Parenchyma organized as:

  • Cord type: anastomosing cords of cells around capillaries (most endocrine glands, adrenal cortex, adenohypophysis)
  • Follicle type: cells form spherical follicles around colloid (thyroid gland)

Hypophysis recognition

Two distinct lobes separate in section (artifact). Adenohypophysis: cords of cells, acidophils (pink) + basophils (blue) + chromophobes (pale). Neurohypophysis: axon terminals, few nuclei (pituicytes = glial), Herring bodies (storage granules).

Thyroid recognition

Follicles filled with colloid (homogeneous pink material). Follicular cells = simple cuboidal (inactive) → columnar (active). Parafollicular (C) cells between follicles.

Adrenal recognition

Outer cortex (3 zones: glomerulosa/fasciculata/reticularis) + inner medulla (chromaffin cells, dark stain, venous sinuses).

Secretion modes (need to know for oral)

M
Merocrine: pure exocytosis, cell intact after. Most glands. Example: parotid, pancreas.
A
Apocrine: apical cytoplasm pinches off with secretion. Lactating mammary gland. Decapitation secretion under EM.
H
Holocrine: entire cell dies, becomes product. Sebaceous glands. Central cells are dead/degenerating.
Connective Tissue Proper

Connective tissue — key features

Three components: cells + fibers + ground substance (ECM).

Loose CT: many cells (fibroblasts, mast cells, macrophages), few fibers, abundant ground substance. Widely spaced fibers.

Dense CT: few cells, many fibers. Regular = parallel fibers (tendons, ligaments); irregular = random orientation (dermis).

No epithelium → no apical-basal polarity, no tight junctions. CT always has ECM. Fibroblasts are the resident cells.

Fibers to recognize

Collagen: thick, wavy, pink (H&E), inelastic, strong. Type I = CT/bone; II = cartilage; III = reticular.

Elastic: thinner, refractile, stretchy. Stain with orcein (black/purple). In aorta, ligaments.

Cartilage

Cartilage — universal features

Avascular, aneural, alymphatic. Chondrocytes in lacunae. Surrounded by ECM.

Three cell types: chondrogenic cells (precursors, spindle) → chondroblasts (active, basophilic) → chondrocytes (mature, in lacunae).

Isogenous groups: clusters of chondrocytes from same parent cell (interstitial growth). Visible as 2–8 cells sharing a territory.

Territorial matrix: immediately around lacuna, rich in proteoglycans → darker/basophilic. Interterritorial matrix: between lacunae, richer in collagen.

Perichondrium = outer fibrous layer (fibroblasts) + inner chondrogenic layer. Absent in articular cartilage and fibrocartilage.

Three types to distinguish

TypeKey recognitionLocation
HyalineGlassy, blue-grey matrix (collagen II invisible). Perichondrium present. Isogenous groups. Most common.Tracheal rings, costal cartilage, articular surfaces, fetal skeleton
ElasticMatrix laced with visible elastic fibers (pink threads in H&E, darker with orcein). More flexible. Folding artifacts in section.Ear (pinna), epiglottis, larynx (some cartilages)
FibrocartilageDense type I collagen bundles visible as coarse pink fibers. Chondrocytes in short columns (no real perichondrium). No glassy matrix.Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis, menisci
Mnemonic: HEF — Hyaline (glass), Elastic (ears/epiglottis), Fibro (force-bearing).
Bone

Bone — key features

Vascularized (unlike cartilage). Matrix mineralized (Ca/PO₄). Collagen type I.

Four cell types

  • Osteoprogenitor: mesenchyme-derived, mitotic, in periosteum/endosteum/Haversian canals.
  • Osteoblasts: cuboidal-columnar, basophilic (lots of RER), secrete osteoid. Arrayed on bone surface.
  • Osteocytes: trapped osteoblasts in lacunae. Flat nucleus. Processes in canaliculi connecting adjacent cells.
  • Osteoclasts: huge, multinucleated (up to 50 nuclei), acidophilic. Sit in Howship's lacunae = active resorption.
Mnemonic: "Progenitors Build Cages, Clasts Demolish"

Compact bone — osteon structure

Recognize: concentric lamellae around a central Haversian canal (blood vessels) = one osteon (Haversian system).

  • Haversian canal: longitudinal, has capillaries, lined by osteoprogenitor cells.
  • Volkmann's canals: transverse, connect Haversian canals to periosteum/marrow.
  • Interstitial lamellae: remnants of old remodeled osteons between current ones.
  • Outer/inner circumferential lamellae: under periosteum / above endosteum.
Canaliculi visible as fine radiating lines from each lacuna. Counting lamellae ~ approximates bone age.

Spongy vs compact bone

Compact: dense, osteons visible. Spongy/trabecular: network of trabeculae, spaces = marrow. Epiphyses = mostly spongy inside; diaphysis = hollow compact cylinder.

Intramembranous ossification

Direct. No cartilage template. Flat bones (frontal, parietal, mandible, clavicle).

1
Mesenchymal cells aggregate (highly vascularized zone)
2
Directly differentiate into osteoblasts → secrete osteoid (unmineralized matrix)
3
Collagen fibers random → woven bone forms (primary spongiosa)
4
Ca²⁺ deposits → mineralization. Osteoblasts become osteocytes.
5
Periosteum forms at surface from remaining mesenchyme
6
Appositional deposition → compact bone OR hematopoietic tissue → cancellous bone
What you see: woven spicules/trabeculae of bone rimmed by osteoblasts, surrounded by vascular mesenchyme.

Endochondral ossification — the big one

Indirect. Uses hyaline cartilage template. Most long and short bones.

1
Hyaline cartilage model grows (appositionally + interstitially)
2
Chondrocytes in diaphysis center hypertrophy → lacunae enlarge → matrix calcifies
3
Perichondrium vascularizes → chondrogenic cells → osteoprogenitor → perichondrium → periosteum
4
Hypertrophic chondrocytes secrete VEGF → vascular invasion
5
Osteoblasts deposit bone → subperiosteal bone collar (by intramembranous!)
6
Hypertrophic chondrocytes die → empty confluent lacunae → future marrow cavity
7
Osteoclasts etch holes → periosteal/osteogenic bud enters
8
Osteoblasts deposit bone on calcified cartilage → calcified cartilage/bone complex
9
Osteoclasts resorb complex → enlarging marrow cavity

Intercalated discs = gap junctions + desmosomes + fascia adherens at cell borders in cardiac muscle. Look for dark transverse lines crossing the full fiber width.

Skeletal muscle — architecture

Endomysium: basal lamina + reticular fibers around each fiber.

Perimysium: loose CT around fascicles (bundles).

Epimysium: dense irregular CT around whole muscle.

Sarcomere bands (H&E)

A band (dark/anisotropic) = myosin thick filaments. Width unchanged during contraction.

I band (light) = actin only. Narrows during contraction.

H band: center of A, myosin tails only. Disappears on full contraction.

Z disk: bisects I band, anchors actin. Moves closer during contraction.

M line: bisects H band.

Mnemonic: "I Am Healthy, Mighty Z" (I band → A band → H zone → M line → Z disk)
Nervous Tissue

Neurons — recognition

Large cells, 5–150 µm. Prominent nucleus (large, spherical, central, finely dispersed chromatin + visible nucleolus) surrounded by cytoplasm with Nissl bodies (basophilic clumps = stacked RER + polyribosomes).

Axon hillock: cone-shaped, no Nissl bodies (only SER). Key LM landmark.

Morphological types

  • Multipolar: 1 axon + ≥2 dendrites. Most common. Motor neurons, Purkinje cells.
  • Bipolar: 1 axon + 1 dendrite. Retina, olfactory epithelium, inner ear.
  • Pseudounipolar: 1 process splits into 2. Dorsal root ganglia (sensory PNS).
Nissl bodies = RER = protein synthesis. Absent at axon hillock. Chromatolysis (Nissl dissolves) = nerve injury sign.

Peripheral nerve structure

Endoneurium: around each axon + myelin sheath.

Perineurium: around each fascicle (bundle of axons).

Epineurium: dense CT around whole nerve.

Myelin sheath

Schwann cell wraps around axon (PNS). Gaps = nodes of Ranvier. Segment between = internode. Allows saltatory conduction.

In H&E: myelin appears as empty space (dissolved) or pale ring. In osmium tetroxide: myelin stains black.

CNS vs PNS grey matter

Spinal cord grey matter

H-shaped grey matter. Large motor neurons in anterior horn (ventral), pseudounipolar sensory in dorsal root ganglia.

Ganglia

PNS. Neuron cell bodies surrounded by satellite cells (pale halo around soma). No synapses visible at this level.

Cerebellum recognition

Three layers: outer molecular layer (sparse cells) → Purkinje cell layer (huge flask-shaped neurons in single row) → granule layer (densely packed small dark cells).
